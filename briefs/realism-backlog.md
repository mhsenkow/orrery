# ORRERY — realism

**800 items.** Generated from `scripts/realism.mjs` — edit that file, not this one, then run `node scripts/realism.mjs`.

Two passes. The first 400 items are the pipeline: fields, materials, water, sky, the mesh, the pixel map, the budget. Seven rendering faults were found and fixed while writing that pass; those are listed first, because they are the shape of the rest.

The second 400 is the photograph. SimEarth painted biomes as a palette and let you hold the disc. The modern claim is that the disc is Earth — the same object DSCOVR photographs, the same one in the 2002 Blue Marble — and that you can still hold it, still push sliders, still walk in. `uEarth` currently turns the vegetation mix down so the result is quieter rather than truer; `seedEarthBiosphere` paints life by latitude belt; `calibrate.mjs` asserts climate scalars and has never asserted a colour. This pass is what has to be true for the globe in your hands to be Earth itself.

Kind: **293** picture, **230** mechanism, **277** instrument or lever. Effort is S/M/L. Impact is 1–3.

## Fixed in this pass

- **Two fields shared one byte in a filtered texture.** `uField0.a` carried `sedQ + cldQ * 16` and the atlas was sampled `LINEAR`. Hardware interpolation between two packed bytes runs straight across the nibble boundary, so `mod(packed, 16.0)` returned nonsense wherever cloud cover crossed any of its sixteen steps. The visible symptom was a tan sediment contour stair-stepping across four kilometres of open ocean — a set of terraced bands over the entire sea that read as a broken terrain layer. Cloud, sediment, intertidal, precipitation and river discharge now have their own channels across three atlases.
- **The atmosphere shell was never tonemapped.** The surface runs `tonemap(col * uExposure)`. The shell computed an unbounded radiance, wrote `o = vec4(col, 1.0)` and was additively blended on top, so the sunward limb clipped to flat white and painted a fat ring of blown sky beyond the disc. It now shares the same curve and the same exposure and writes a premultiplied coverage instead of a constant alpha.
- **The moon was an unlit disc.** It was drawn with `healthProg`, whose entire fragment shader is `o = vec4(uCol, 0.85)`, with the phase faked by scaling a constant colour. There is now a small-body shader with a real terminator, maria, craters, Lommel-Seeliger backscatter, an opposition surge and limb darkening.
- **The sun glint was a white blob at every angle.** A Blinn-Phong lobe wide enough to survive a calm sea plus a second wider lobe on top. It is a GGX microfacet lobe now, with roughness driven by the wind field and its energy clamped, plus a Fresnel sky reflection that is most of why a real ocean is not a flat tinted disc.
- **The cloud shell was a quarter of the grid resolution, sampled nearest.** Coverage came from `dirToCell` onto `6 x 17 x 17` vertices and was then used to displace the shell radially, so every band edge became a hard terrace with a visible ledge on the limb. The shell now matches N, samples bilinearly, and takes its lift from convective type rather than raw coverage.
- **Two noise patterns were anchored to the world normal.** The sea-ice floe pattern and the lightning flash were hashed from `N.xy` — the *world* normal — so both swam across the surface as the planet turned. Object space now, so the pack ice stays where it is.
- **There was nothing between 250 km and one metre.** A cell is about 250 km across and the surface was one interpolated colour with no sub-cell variation at all. Two octaves of object-space value noise now drive an albedo mottle and a sun-direction relief term, faded out by `fwidth(vObj)` before either period reaches the sampling limit.

## Where the picture actually is

- **The picture and the model are computed twice, differently.** `refreshColours` is 320 lines of per-cell colour logic on the CPU — depth ramps, ice leads, whitecaps, SST shift, rain darkening, blooms, sediment, reefs, plumes, storm trails, biomes, ash, build, guild tint. The fragment shader then computes a second, overlapping set of effects from the field atlas. `describeCell` in `present.js` computes a third for the pixel map. Three descriptions of the same cell, in three files, that drift apart every time one of them is touched.
- **There is no level of detail anywhere.** `buildLatticeArrays` emits 786,432 vertices at N=64 and subd 2 and draws all of them from the orrery table and from a coastline alike. The field atlas has no mipmaps. The local map redraws every cell of the patch, with `describeCell`, on every frame. Nothing in the renderer knows how far away it is except the two detail-fade terms added in this pass.
- **The field atlas is wrong in a band around all twelve cube edges.** `vFieldUV` maps cell `(f, i, j)` into a `6N x N` strip with `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`, so a fragment near the edge of one face blends with the edge of a face that is somewhere else entirely on the planet. Every field the surface shader reads is corrupt in a one-cell border around every face.
- **Moisture is pinned at its ceiling over two thirds of the planet.** A 120-tick probe of the terra ruleset returns `moist` with an area-weighted mean of 0.775 and 69.4% of cells at or above 0.985. A field saturated over most of its domain cannot express a gradient, so cloud formation, soil colour, vegetation and haze are all reading a constant across most of the world.
- **Nothing on the globe moves between two frames.** `presentAdvance` gives the view a clock independent of the 10 kyr sim tick and only the local map uses it. `uTime` reaches the surface shader for the lightning hash and the aurora pulse and nothing else. A paused ORRERY and a running one are almost the same image from orbit.
- **Only three fields are interpolated across the tick.** `life`, `temp` and `ice` are lerped between `prev` and current. Cloud, moisture, NPP, wind, sediment, precipitation and discharge all snap, and that snap is the single loudest motion artefact in the frame.
- **Every flow in the model is drawn as 1600 one-frame line segments.** `fillFlowStreaks` emits `prev -> pos` per particle at a constant alpha with no taper, no lifetime and no width — `gl.lineWidth` is clamped to 1 on essentially every implementation. And because `stepFlow` reads the velocity at the containing cell centre, every particle entering a cell moves in exactly the same direction, so the paths collapse onto a handful of shared streamlines with visible corners at cell boundaries.
- **There is no shadow of any kind on the planet.** Terrain does not shade the ground behind it, clouds do not cast onto the surface — `col *= 1.0 - cloudF * landMask * 0.38` darkens the cell that is under the cloud rather than the cell the sun projects it onto — and nothing standing on the surface casts anything. The one occlusion term is `pow(1 - heightF, 1.4)`, a guess with no direction in it.
- **The pixel map has no visual hierarchy.** The base fill, the detail pass, the biome texture, the life stamps, the buildings, the grid, the rivers, the weather and the plumes all draw at roughly the same alpha and the same density, with more than sixty separate `rgba(...)` literals between them. A savanna cell and a river and a herd of animals put down the same amount of ink, so nothing reads as figure and nothing reads as ground.
- **The two views disagree about the light.** The globe computes lighting in a fragment shader from `uSun` and a surface normal; the map calls `cellLight` and `applyLight`. They disagree on direction, on colour and on where the terminator is, and both panels are on screen at the same time showing the same place.
- **One byte per channel, for everything.** Every field the shader reads is `UNSIGNED_BYTE`. `heightF = clamp(0.5 + (h - sea) * 2.2, 0, 1)` throws away everything below and above roughly four kilometres of elevation and quantises what is left to 1/255 — on the field that drives the water mask, the shoreline, the valley darkening and the new sediment gate.
- **The renderer is unmeasured and untested.** There is no GPU timer query, no frame budget, no device tier, no per-pass cost, and no rendered-reference test of any kind. `npm test` runs 59 assertions about physics and zero about the picture, which means every item in this document can be silently reverted by one optimisation and nothing will fail.

## Where Earth actually is

- **uEarth makes Earth quieter, not truer.** `pop = lifeGreen * (1.0 - uEarth * 0.65)` and the vegetation mix `mix(0.58, 0.30, uEarth)` dim the biosphere on the one world everyone can fact-check. Quiet is not a photograph. Real canopy albedo is about 0.08; the render tints toward a mid green that reads as grass from orbit, then turns it down so it will not pop.
- **Continents are climate belts.** `seedEarthBiosphere` paints life by `|lat|` and moisture; `primeEarthMoisture` hard-codes a desert belt between 0.28 and 0.50. The Sahara–Amazon–Siberia triad exists as climate and not as geography, so a generated Earth is a diagram of Hadley cells wearing terrain.
- **Ocean colour is two triples.** `ocean(d) => [10+42d, 48+72d, 96+100d]` mixed with a depth lerp to `[22,45,75]`. Open-ocean albedo is about 0.06; the blue in a Blue Marble is almost all sky. The water is a bright game ocean, then bloom and sediment are painted on top.
- **Daisyworld is a luminance filter.** `blackDaisy` and `whiteDaisy` are real fields, and `uDaisy` then guesses them back from `daisyBW = max(smoothstep(0.72, 0.9, lum), ...)`. The SimEarth tutorial is the one world that should be two albedos doing work, and it is a mix factor on the Earth shader.
- **Calibration has never seen a colour.** `calibrate.mjs` allows landFrac 0.22–0.38, iceFrac 0–0.30, meanTemp 0.38–0.62. It does not assert ocean chromaticity, cloud distribution, night-light geography, or a limb width. A passing Earth can still be a lime continent with a fat halo.
- **N is 64 in code and 96 in the ladder.** `sphere.js` exports `N = 64` (~157 km/cell). `N_LADDER` calls 96 the shipped default. This document, and several others, still say 250 km. The grid that is supposed to hold a coastline cannot agree with itself about how fine it is.
- **The hold is a joystick axis.** `scaleXR` clamps 0.07–0.95; `orreryTable.js` tints six slots from meanLife and meanTemp. Relative radii are unused; a thumb cannot occlude the limb; the live world is duplicated onto the table as a second bead. The product is named for a table of worlds you hold.
- **Gaia is a HUD on stub variables.** `TIPPING` names Amazon, AMOC, monsoon; `W._amoc` is `W.conveyor ?? W._amoc ?? 0.7` and `_monsoon` is a constant 0.5. Resilience is a popcount of eight life classes. The metabolism does not show on the disc, so the SimEarth thesis — the planet as a face — is a panel.

## Light, exposure and the single tone curve — 28

_The planet surface runs `tonemap(col * uExposure)` with an ACES curve. Until this pass the atmosphere shell did not: it computed an unbounded radiance, wrote `o = vec4(col, 1.0)`, and was additively blended over an image that had already been mapped — so the sunward limb clipped to flat white and painted a fat ring of blown sky several tenths of a radius wide. The moon was worse: it was drawn with `healthProg`, whose entire fragment shader is `o = vec4(uCol, 0.85)`, so the nearest object to the planet was an unlit beige circle. The shell and the moon now share `_exposure` with the surface. Everything else that draws over the planet — entities, meshes, streaks, rims, the interior stack — still does not, and every one of them is a place where two differently-scaled images meet._

## The field pipeline: precision, packing and what the GPU is given — 28

_Two RGBA8 atlases, `6N x N`, carried eight scalars to the shader and one of the eight was wrong. `uField0.a` bit-packed sediment into the low nibble and cloud into the high nibble — in a texture sampled `LINEAR`. Hardware interpolation between two packed bytes runs across the nibble boundary, so `mod(packed, 16.0)` returned garbage wherever cloud cover crossed any of its sixteen steps, and the visible symptom was a tan sediment contour stair-stepping across four kilometres of open ocean. That is fixed here by splitting into a third atlas — cloud is 8-bit, sediment and intertidal have their own channels, and precipitation and river discharge reach the surface shader for the first time. What is left is the rest of the pipeline: every channel is still 8-bit, still uploaded by a full `NC` CPU loop every tick, and still nearest-in-face at the six cube seams._

## The four missing decades between a cell and a footprint — 30

_A cell is about 250 km across at N=64. The renderer draws it, the local map draws it, and between the two there is nothing: no material, no sub-cell variation, no reason for one square of savanna to look different from the next. ## Shape: the mesh, the seams and the silhouette — 28

_`rebuildGeometry` displaces a `6 x (N*subd+1)^2` lattice by `W.h`, clamps Earth relief to 0.028, blends 40% bilinear against 60% piecewise-constant neighbour mean, then recomputes vertex normals over the whole index buffer and welds the twelve cube edges. ## The budget, the tiers and the guard — 28

_Everything in this document costs fill rate or CPU time on a device that might be a phone or a standalone headset. `refreshColours` is a 300-line per-cell loop over 24,576 cells; `uploadFieldTextures` is another; `rebuildGeometry` is a third plus a full normal rebuild. ## The thin blue line — 29

## Sequencing

1. **Make the picture measurable before making it better.** `gputime`, `imgtest`, `visreg`. A GPU timer query, a debug overlay showing per-pass cost, and a handful of rendered reference frames diffed with a perceptual tolerance. Everything below this line costs milliseconds, and a renderer with no visual test is one clever optimisation away from losing all of it. The field inspector belongs here too: every artefact fixed in this pass took hours to find and would have taken minutes with a way to look at a channel.
2. **Fix the pipeline, then stop computing the picture twice.** `nopack` is done. `seamfix`, `floatfield`, `gpubasis`, then `gpucolour`. A one-texel gutter kills the corruption around all twelve cube edges; float channels give height and temperature back their dynamic range; the tangent frame on the GPU unblocks every direction-aware effect in the document; and moving `refreshColours` into the fragment shader deletes the largest CPU cost in the renderer while making every effect it performs sub-cell instead of per-cell.
3. **Then light it properly, once.** `tonecurve`, `hdrbuf`, `terrshadow`. One linear HDR target, every pass writing into it, one resolve. Bloom, adaptation and correct additive blending all fall out of that, and so does the end of the class of bug this pass spent most of its time on — two differently-scaled images meeting at a limb.
4. **Then give the surface a material and a scale.** `material`, `lodmesh`. A material table selected from rock, soil and life replaces one albedo and one roughness heuristic; a quadtree LOD replaces 786,432 vertices drawn at every distance. Together they are what make the four decades between a cell and a footprint into something rather than nothing.
5. **Then make the water and the sky behave.** `watercol`, `swell`, `cloudtop`. Depth-dependent extinction with a real spectrum replaces the two-colour ramp and gets turquoise-to-indigo for free; a swell field that travels gives every coast a surf line connected to a storm somewhere else; a cloud top height turns one coverage scalar into a sky with layers, anvils, shadows and a limb profile.
6. **Then make it move.** `globeclock`. Interpolate every field across the tick, advect the cloud texture with the wind, give the tracers a lifetime and a taper and sample their velocity between cells rather than at the centre, and let the tide move the shoreline against the presentation clock. This is the category with the worst ratio of effort to felt improvement in the whole document, because almost nothing has been done in it.
7. **Then rebuild the pixel map around a hierarchy.** `imgdata`, `hierarchy`, `sharedlight`. Composite into a typed array instead of issuing tens of thousands of `fillRect` calls; give every layer an explicit tier and an alpha budget; and share one description of a cell and one lighting model with the globe. The map is the half of the product with the most ground to make up, and every item in it is blocked on those three.
8. **Then let the other 119 worlds look like themselves.** `worldshader`. One shader with an `uEarth` branch is serving a catalogue that includes gas giants, ice moons, lava worlds, hazy worlds and free-floaters. Rings, a real host star in the sky, a locked eyeball ocean and true relative radii are the four items that would do most to make the catalogue a claim you can see.
9. **Then make the disc a photograph of Earth.** `bluemarble`, `truecolor`, `eoref`. Dark ocean, dark forest, pale desert, white ice, white cloud; albedo numbers instead of a biome palette; a shelf of NASA stills the render has to sit beside. `uEarth` as a dimmer comes off. Until a stranger calls the boxed seed Earth, nothing in the second pass has landed.
10. **Then the limb and the coast, which are how you know.** `limbvol`, `coastline`. Scale height over radius is 8.5/6371, not a halo thicker than Everest; the strand is fractal, asymmetric, and moves with the tide. These two edges are the whole of a Blue Marble at thumbnail size.
11. **Then the year, the lights and the face.** `phenology`, `lights`, `gaiaface`. A green wave that travels, VIIRS geography instead of `vD.b`, tipping elements as places. This is SimEarth DNA on a planet that photographs.
12. **Then one scale you can hold.** `zoomspan`, `holdfeel`, `groundcam`. Table, disc, ISS, country, patch, stand — one kilometres number, true relative radii on the table, a horizon when you walk in. The thesis is a descent, not two apps and a clamp.

The through-line of the first pass: the picture has never been measured. There is no GPU timer, no frame budget, no device tier, no reference frame and no test — so a nibble-packed field could corrupt the ocean of every world for as long as it liked, an untonemapped shell could clip the limb to white, and the nearest object to the planet could be an unlit beige circle, all at once, in a codebase with 59 passing assertions about physics. Fix the instruments first. Then fix the pipeline so the picture is computed once instead of three times.

The through-line of the second: SimEarth let you hold a diagram. The modern extrapolation has to be Earth itself — dark ocean, thin blue limb, a year that travels, lights that mean cities — and still a toy you can tilt. `bluemarble` and `eoref` first, because a photograph without a photograph to sit beside is taste. Then `limbvol` and `coastline`, the two edges that survive a thumbnail. Then `phenology`, `lights` and `gaiaface`, which are SimEarth DNA on a planet that photographs. Then `zoomspan` and `holdfeel`, because the thesis is a descent and a hand.
