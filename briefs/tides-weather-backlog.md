# ORRERY — tides and weather

**200 items.** Generated from `scripts/tides-weather.mjs` — edit that file, not this one, then run `node scripts/tides-weather.mjs`.

A focused batch on the two systems the last review flagged: **tides, which are absent entirely**, and **weather, which is present in the code and invisible on the planet.** 80 items on tides and the Moon, 120 on the atmosphere.

Kind: **112** dynamics, **43** picture, **45** instrument or lever. Effort is S/M/L. Impact is 1–3.

## Where these two actually are

- **Tides do not exist.** `W.moon` is `{ mass, distance, formed }`. `setMoon` scales `rotationPeriod` by `0.85 + 0.15 * distance`, toggles `obliquityWander`, and issues a receipt reading "Axis stabilised · tides resume". Nothing resumes — there is no tidal potential, no bulge, no range field, no intertidal zone, and no coastal inundation anywhere in the codebase.
- **The Moon is a grey sphere.** `render.js` draws it on the ecliptic, sized by the cube root of its mass. It has no phases, no orbit, no rise or set, no eclipse, and it does not appear in the Local tier sky at all.
- **Day length is a lookup, not a consequence.** `dayLengthDays(ageYr)` in `time.js` fits Earth’s recession history. A world with a different moon, a different ocean depth or a different continental configuration gets Earth’s answer, because there is no torque being computed.
- **Winds are prescribed, not solved.** `computeWinds` assigns `v` from three latitude bands and `u` from three more, multiplies both by `cor = 1 / rotationPeriod`, adds a small temperature-gradient term and a flat 0.7 continental drag. There is no pressure field. Nothing can reorganise, because there is nothing for the circulation to be a solution to.
- **Spin changes wind speed, never the banding.** That `cor` multiplier is the whole of the rotation response. The actual physics — the Rhines scale setting how many cells a planet has, and the Held–Hou result setting how wide they are — means a faster rotator should get more and narrower bands. That is the reorganisation the player is looking for and it cannot happen.
- **Advection is zonal only.** `windV` now feeds upwelling in `ecology.js`, precipitation in `hydro.js` and wind magnitude in the renderer — real progress. But `advect` still reads only `windU`, so heat, moisture, ash and dust are carried east–west and never north–south.
- **Clouds are a diagnostic haze.** `clouds[c] = clouds[c] * 0.85 + form * 0.5 + ash[c] * 0.2` where `form = moist * (1.1 - temp) * 0.7 + precip * 0.4`. It is a correlation, not a process. It cannot produce a cyclone, a front, a squall line, an ITCZ or a storm track, because none of those are functions of the local state.
- **The picture has moved ahead of the physics.** Cloud typing, lightning and cloud shadows all landed in `render.js`, and each is driven by the wrong variable because the right one does not exist. Type comes from coverage rather than stability; lightning is one global storm count flashing the whole night side; the shadow is a uniform mean whose own comment says so. The renderer is doing its best with a haze field.
- **There is no weather instrument.** `instruments.js` has ten readouts — cores, Keeling, Sepkoski, Whittaker, transit spectra, the redox gauge — and not one of them is synoptic. `inspectCell` returns fifteen fields and no atmospheric structure among them.
- **`localWeather` paints, it does not cause.** It adds 0.25 to moisture and 0.3 to cloud inside a brush radius. It is the only weather verb in a toolset of twenty-seven.

## Making the dynamics honest — 18

_`computeWinds` assigns `v` from three latitude bands and `u` from three more, multiplies both by `cor = 1 / rotationPeriod`, adds a temperature-gradient nudge and a 0.7 continental drag. There is no pressure field, so there is nothing that can reorganise — which is exactly why spin changes wind speed and never changes the weather._

| 81 | **A pressure field, and wind derived from it** <br>gives `press` | This is the item the entire weather half of this document rests on. `computeWinds` assigns velocities from latitude bands; until wind is the response to a pressure gradient, nothing about the circulation can reorganise in response to anything, and the spin lever will keep changing wind speed without changing weather. | Sim | L | 3 |
| 82 | **Geostrophic balance** <br>needs `press` | Away from the surface, the pressure gradient force and the Coriolis force very nearly cancel and the wind blows along the isobars rather than across them. ## Sequencing

1. **The pressure field, first and alone.** `press` is the item everything in the weather half waits on — 8 items directly and effectively all 120 indirectly. Until wind is the response to a gradient rather than a lookup by latitude, no lever can reorganise anything and every visual item below is decorating a fiction. Take the two cheap correctness fixes with it: `adv`, and reading `windV` at all.
2. **The tidal field, which is independent.** `tide` → `range` → `shore` → `intertidal` is a clean four-item chain that touches nothing in the atmosphere and unblocks 30 items across biology, geology, the coastline and the catalogue. It can be built in parallel with the pressure work by a different person, and it makes the Moon lever honest for the first time.
3. **Then the structures the field implies.** `rhines`, `baro`, `convect`, `itcz`, `cyclone`, `front`. This is where storms, jets, the ITCZ and storm tracks come from. `baro` in particular is non-negotiable: baroclinic instability is the actual source of midlatitude weather and no amount of cloud tuning substitutes for it.
4. **Then make it visible.** `chart` and `cloudvis`. A synoptic map that redraws when you change spin, and cloud banding you can see without an overlay. These two answer the original complaint directly — that the sim has the circulation and the player cannot read it.
5. **Then the levers land on their own.** `spinfix` and the receipt chain. Once the physics reorganises and the map shows it, the spin and tilt tools become the most interesting things in the toolset without any further work on the tools themselves.

The two halves are genuinely independent until the very end, where storm surge needs both a storm and a tide. That makes this a good batch to split across two people.
