# ORRERY — the next 200

**200 items.** Generated from `scripts/next.mjs` — edit that file, not this one, then run `node scripts/next.mjs`.

The fifth backlog. The first covered the systems, the second the worlds, the third the biosphere, the fourth the god layer. Three and four are now substantially built. This one is the honest account of what that left behind: **the model is a long way ahead of the picture, the machine, and the audience.**

Kind: **45** picture, **47** engine, **37** simulation depth, **71** audience. Effort is S/M/L. Impact is 1–3.

## Where it actually is

- **The simulation is done, and it is good.** Deep time on the real ICS chart with an adaptive tick. A redox tower of metabolic guilds with electron donors and acceptors as fields. Carbon split into reservoirs with oxygen from burial. A trait vector, a live phylogeny, speciation and the major transitions. NPP, Whittaker biomes, trophic structure. Extinction with named kill mechanisms. Alien biospheres with photon gates. Ten instruments that read all of it.
- **The god layer is done, and it is good.** A brush with a profile, kilometre radius, masks, snap, symmetry and undo. Sculpting that edits crust and plate poles rather than elevation. Climate levers that reach orbits and magnetospheres. Guild seeding, an organism designer, refuges, culls. Parameterised impactors with aftermath chains. Receipts, forecasts, delayed attribution, a power meter. Thermodynamic costs with scarcity modes. Genesis, scenarios, a campaign, a shelf, and a civilisation that notices you.
- **The picture did not keep up.** Surface colour is still one interpolated RGB per vertex written by `refreshColours`, with the fragment shader reverse-engineering biome type from `biome.g - max(biome.r, biome.b)`. Organisms are still sixteen SVG paths on camera-facing quads. The atmosphere is an excellent hand-tuned analytic approximation that has reached its ceiling. The model knows far more about this planet than it is allowed to show.
- **The machine did not keep up.** 24,576 cells and fifteen coupled subsystems, serial, on the main thread, behind a guard that silently drops ticks when a frame runs long. No workers, no GPGPU, no transform feedback. Sixty-eight surviving `Math.random()` calls despite three seeded streams sitting in `W`. No tests of any kind. A model this elaborate that cannot reproduce its own run cannot be trusted with the claims it is making.
- **The audience did not keep up.** Twenty-seven tools in one dock, fifteen coupled systems, a redox tower, an ICS ribbon, and a first run that opens on Earth mid-Holocene with no proposition. `tips.js` and a `CAMPAIGN` array are the entire teaching layer. The quality of the simulation is invisible to anyone who cannot get to it.
- **Two systems are still absent.** Civilisation is a `build` scalar and a prayer queue — the last major system SimEarth had that this does not, and the one the carbon cycle has been waiting for since the beginning. And nothing in the build knows how to end: no finale, no ending, no artefact the player leaves with.
- **The catalogue is still a promise.** `catalogue-rules.js` maps 120 real bodies onto five invented templates with a colour tint and clamped parameters. The star is not an object, tidal locking is a large `rotationPeriod`, and the ice-shell worlds run a surface heightfield model that cannot represent them.

## Rendering: the surface — 15

Everything the fragment shader does on top of it — glint, canopy glow, night side — is a clever guess made from that one triple. ## The engine: making it fast — 14

_24,576 cells, roughly forty Float32Arrays, fifteen coupled subsystems, all serial JavaScript on the main thread, behind a `if (elapsed > 12) { simAcc = 0; break; }` guard that silently drops ticks when a frame runs long. The model is now good enough that the honest constraint on it is arithmetic throughput._

| # | Item | Detail | Kind | Effort | Impact |

## Ocean and atmosphere, properly — 13

_The two largest admissions in `briefs/model-limits.md`. Winds are prescribed three-cell banding with a terrain nudge; the ocean is a surface scalar with a conveyor flag. Almost every remaining climate question the player can ask is answered by something the model does not have._

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 83 | **A real ocean with layers** <br>gives `ocean3d` | The ocean is a surface temperature and a conveyor flag. | 84 | **Wind-driven gyres from wind stress** <br>needs `ocean3d` | Surface currents as the actual response to the curl of the wind stress against continental geometry, rather than a global conveyor toggle. ## Sequencing

Five blocks, in order. Each is worth shipping alone.

1. **Make it reproducible first.** `det`, seeded streams, a golden run, conservation assertions, a headless harness. Everything below is built on sand until a run can be repeated. It is also the cheapest block here and the one that unblocks batch experiments, sharing and rewind-the-tape all at once.
2. **Then send the fields to the GPU.** `gbuf` and `gpgpu` are the same architectural move from two directions — stop collapsing the model into a colour, and stop iterating it in JavaScript. Fifteen rendering items and most of the performance items are waiting behind them.
3. **Then the atmosphere and the bodies.** `scatter`, `hdr`, `mesh`, `grammar`. Physical scattering is what makes every world in the catalogue look like itself, and generated morphology is what closes the widest gap in the codebase — between what `evolve.js` knows about an organism and what the player is shown of it.
4. **Then teach it.** `reveal`, the campaign, the authored first run, and watching somebody use it without helping. The simulation is now good enough that the binding constraint is whether anyone can reach it.
5. **Then civilisation and the ending.** `city` and `end`. The last major system, and the thing that turns four billion years of play into something the player takes away. Both are large; neither should start before the four blocks above are done.

One thing is not on this list and should be decided before any of it: **what this is for.** Toy, teaching tool, scientific instrument, art piece — it is currently all four, the briefs argue for different ones, and almost every prioritisation above resolves differently depending on the answer.
