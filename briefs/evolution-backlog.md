# ORRERY — evolution & fidelity backlog

**200 items.** Generated from `scripts/evolution.mjs` — edit that file, not this one, then run `node scripts/evolution.mjs`.

This is the third backlog. The first covers the systems SimEarth had; the second covers the 120 real worlds to put them on. This one covers the thing both of those exist to serve: **making the evolution of life on a planet something you can watch happen, over deep time, and believe.**

Axis: **101** simulation truth, **36** what the player sees, **63** both. Effort is S/M/L. Impact is 1–3, where 3 means the thesis does not land without it.

## Where the prototype actually is

Read honestly, so the list below has something to be measured against.

- **The clock.** 200 years a tick, uniform. Eras are named every 48,000 years from an eight-branch `if` chain in `chronicle.js`. There is no geologic timescale, and the Hadean is 24 silent warmup ticks in `generate()` with the biosphere paused so the globe does not turn green.
- **Life.** One `Float32Array` of density and one `Uint8Array` of class index, against eight hard-coded classes from `prokaryote` to `mammal` in `sim/bio.js`. Advancement is gated on `W.year >= (unlockedClass + 1) * 5000` — a year counter, not a condition. Trophic structure is three global scalars. There is no genome, no lineage, no tree, and no extinction that is not a dieback in density.
- **Chemistry.** Seven gases as volume mixing ratios, a log-forcing greenhouse, a one-line silicate weathering term, and photosynthesis as a pair of constants (`0.00000008` and `0.000000025`) applied to total biomass. Oxygen accumulates directly from photosynthesis rather than from carbon burial, which is the wrong mechanism in a way that matters.
- **The good parts.** Plate tectonics, boundary classification, hotspots, erosion with sediment routing, a real water budget, three-cell circulation, obliquity, sea ice separated from land ice, a dust cycle, tsunamis, and a 120-world catalogue that maps real bodies onto playable rulesets. The geosphere and hydrosphere are genuinely ahead of the biosphere, and that gap is the whole opportunity.
- **The picture.** WebGL2, a cube-sphere with vertex colours from per-ruleset colour functions, an analytic atmosphere rim, a translucent cloud shell, and sixteen SVG sprites on camera-facing quads. It is well made and it is a diagram of a planet rather than a planet.

## The shape of a good run

If all two hundred items landed, this is what a four-and-a-half-billion-year Earth run should feel like — the arc the list is arranged to produce.

| Interval | What the player is watching |
|---|---|
| **Accretion → 4.4 Ga** | A magma ocean cooling under a dim sun, a Moon torn out of the side of it, and the first liquid water condensing onto crust that keeps being destroyed. Nothing is alive and the geology is doing all the work. |
| **4.0 → 2.5 Ga** | Hydrogen from cracked rock at the vents. Something starts copying itself. For half a billion years the only visible sign is a faint iridescence on shallow flats — mats, then stromatolites — while a methane haze thickens overhead and the sky is orange. |
| **2.45 → 2.0 Ga** | One lineage learns to split water. Iron drops out of the ocean in bands you will be able to core four billion years later; the haze clears; the sky turns blue for the first time; and most of what was alive dies of the new air. |
| **1.8 → 0.8 Ga** | A billion years where almost nothing happens, and the reason is legible — sulfidic oceans, no trace metals, no phosphorus, a complex cell that exists and cannot afford to become anything. The player has to sit in it, and the isotope curves are flat while they do. |
| **720 → 540 Ma** | Ice to the equator, twice, and a planet that only escapes because volcanoes keep breathing under the lid. Then oxygen climbs again and within a geological instant there are eyes, shells, and things that hunt. |
| **470 → 250 Ma** | Green climbs out of the water and onto the rock, and immediately breaks the planet — weathering triples, CO₂ collapses, the ocean suffocates. Forests bury carbon nothing can digest, oxygen reaches 35%, and the world burns. Then Siberia opens and 81% of everything in the sea is gone. |
| **250 → 66 Ma** | Recovery over ten million years, then the largest land animals the planet will ever carry, then ten kilometres of rock arriving at 20 km/s and two years without a summer. |
| **66 Ma → now** | Grass, ice, the ocean conveyor switching on, and a lineage that starts moving carbon out of the rock and back into the air roughly ten thousand times faster than the thermostat can answer. |

## Metabolism before morphology — 16

_Life is currently a 0–1 scalar with an eight-rung ladder bolted on, and the rungs are body plans. For the first three billion years the interesting variable was not shape, it was which electron went where. Rebuild the biosphere on a redox tower and the Archean stops being a loading screen._

| # | Item | Detail | Axis | Effort | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Rebuild the biosphere on a redox tower** <br>gives `redox` | Replace `LIFE_CLASSES` with metabolic guilds ordered by the reduction potential of the half-reactions they run. A guild is viable in a cell when its donor and acceptor are both present and the free energy yield clears a maintenance threshold; when the last cell running a pathway dies, the pathway is gone until something reinvents it. This one change turns the first three billion years from a waiting room into the main event, and it gives the chemistry a memory. | Sim | L | 3 |
| 14 | **Track the electron donors and acceptors as fields** <br>gives `species` <br>needs `redox` | H₂, H₂S, Fe²⁺, CH₄, NH₄⁺, organic carbon on the donor side; O₂, NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, Fe³⁺, CO₂ on the acceptor side. Ten scalar fields, and every microbial story in Earth history is a competition between them for the same electrons. | Sim | M | 3 |
| 15 | **Growth yield from actual free energy** <br>needs `species` | ΔG for each reaction under local concentration and temperature, converted to a growth yield. Aerobic respiration of glucose releases roughly 2,870 kJ/mol against about 130 for methanogenesis from H₂ and CO₂ — a twenty-fold difference that explains why the world changed shape once oxygen existed. | Sim | M | 3 |
| 16 | **Chemolithoautotrophy first** <br>needs `redox` | Serpentinization of olivine at hydrothermal systems produces H₂ abiotically and continuously. Seed the first guild at vent cells — which the code already flags via `bound[c] === 0` — and let it be genuinely independent of sunlight, because the first biosphere was. | Sim | M | 3 |
| 17 | **Methanogens and the Archean haze** <br>needs `species` `faint` | A methanogen-dominated ocean pushes atmospheric CH₄ to perhaps 1,000 ppm, and above a C/O ratio near 1 the photochemistry produces a Titan-like organic haze that is an *anti*-greenhouse. A biosphere that warms its planet, then veils it, then cools it — Gaia’s first real argument with itself. | Both | M | 3 |
| 18 | **Anoxygenic photosynthesis, in its proper place** <br>needs `species` | Purple and green sulfur bacteria split H₂S rather than H₂O, produce elemental sulfur rather than oxygen, and almost certainly came first because the chemistry is far easier. Give them a few hundred million years of dominance and the surface should be visibly not-green. | Both | M | 3 |
| 19 | **Oxygenic photosynthesis as a single hard invention** <br>gives `oxyphoto` <br>needs `species` | Splitting water requires the Mn₄CaO₅ cluster and a redox potential no other biological reaction reaches. | 20 | **Aerobic respiration and the energy budget for being big** <br>needs `oxyphoto` | Roughly sixteen times the ATP yield of fermentation. | 22 | **Close the nitrogen cycle: nitrification, denitrification, anammox** <br>needs `species` | Fixed nitrogen returns to N₂ through anaerobic pathways, so the ocean’s nitrogen inventory is a balance between two microbial guilds rather than a stock. ## The major evolutionary transitions — 14

_Maynard Smith and Szathmáry’s framing, updated: a handful of one-off changes in how information is stored and inherited, each of which made everything after it possible. These should be contingent, gated, and occasionally never happen at all._

| 30 | **Abiogenesis as a probability, not a start condition** <br>gives `origin` <br>needs `trans` | A per-cell, per-interval chance modulated by the presence of a redox gradient, a mineral surface, a wet–dry cycle and a temperature band. ## Genomes, traits and open-ended evolution — 16

_`lifeClass` is a Uint8 index into a fixed array of eight. That is a taxonomy, not an evolutionary process. Give populations a heritable trait vector and the ladder becomes a tree that you did not author._

| 44 | **Mutation with per-trait rates** <br>needs `traits` | Each trait gets its own mutational variance, because they genuinely differ: pigment shifts easily, body plan does not. ## Ecology: who eats whom — 14

_Three global scalars — herbivore, carnivore, meanLife — stand in for the entire trophic structure of a planet. Almost every legible ecological pattern is a consequence of energy transfer efficiency and body-size scaling, both of which are arithmetic._

| # | Item | Detail | Axis | Effort | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 59 | **Net primary productivity from a real model** <br>gives `npp` | `carryingCapacity()` multiplies insolation by water by nutrients and clamps to 0.55. Replace it with light-use efficiency against absorbed radiation, limited by temperature and water — Monteith’s formulation, or the Miami model as a cheaper first pass. NPP is the currency every other ecological item spends. | Sim | M | 3 |
| 60 | **Trophic pyramids from transfer efficiency** <br>needs `npp` | About 10% of the energy at one trophic level reaches the next. Four levels therefore cost three orders of magnitude, which is why apex predators are rare, why they go extinct first, and why an ecosystem cannot have five of them. Arithmetic, not balancing. | Sim | M | 3 |
| 61 | **The food web as an object with links** <br>needs `npp` `traits` | Two global scalars — `herbivore` and `carnivore` — currently stand in for every trophic relationship on the planet. A sparse link matrix between populations, weighted by body-size ratio and encounter rate, gives you cascades, keystones and secondary extinctions for free. | Sim | L | 3 |
| 62 | **Type II and III functional responses** <br>needs `npp` | Predator intake saturates with prey density because handling takes time, and switches when alternative prey is available. Naive Lotka–Volterra oscillates forever; a saturating response gives you the stable coexistence and the sudden collapses that real systems show. | Sim | M | 2 |
| 63 | **Kleiber’s law** <br>gives `kleiber` <br>needs `traits` | Metabolic rate scales as mass to the three-quarter power, so population density scales as mass to the minus three-quarters and lifespan scales as mass to the quarter. Three of the most robust patterns in biology, from one exponent, and they set how many of anything can be alive at once. | Sim | S | 3 |
| 64 | **Temperature-dependent metabolic rates** <br>needs `kleiber` | Rates scale with an Arrhenius term near 0.65 eV, which means a 10 °C warming roughly doubles metabolic demand. This is why warming oceans lose large animals first and why the tropics have fast turnover — and it couples the biosphere to the climate model in the right direction. | Sim | M | 2 |
| 65 | **Body size limited by oxygen and gravity** <br>needs `kleiber` | Carboniferous dragonflies with 70 cm wingspans needed ~30% atmospheric O₂ because insect tracheae are diffusion-limited. `W.bodyScale` already gestures at this with a linear O₂ term; make it a real diffusion and support-strength constraint that differs between body plans and gravities. | Both | M | 3 |

## Sequencing

Four blocks, in order. Each is worth shipping alone.

1. **Make time honest.** `clock`, `logtime`, `chart`, `faint`. Everything else is unreadable until a tick means a defensible number of years and the era ribbon shows real proportions. Cheapest block on the list and the one that changes the most.
2. **Make life chemical.** `redox`, `species`, `carbon`, `oxyphoto`, and oxygen-from-burial. This is the SimEarth thesis, rebuilt on the modern understanding, and it makes the first three billion years worth playing.
3. **Make life evolve.** `traits`, `spec`, `tree`, plus the ecology and biogeography categories. This is where the ladder becomes a tree you did not author and the player starts seeing things they were not shown.
4. **Make it beautiful.** `pbr`, `scatter`, `hdr`, `mesh`, `grammar`. The rendering work is deliberately last because most of it is a way of *displaying* the systems above — and because scattering and PBR are the two items that make everything already in the engine look like a photograph.

The instruments in category 14 should be built alongside whatever they read, not after. An instrument written at the same time as its system is a design constraint on that system, and a good one.
