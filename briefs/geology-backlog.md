# ORRERY — geology

**482 items.** Generated from `scripts/geology.mjs` — edit that file, not this one, then run `node scripts/geology.mjs`.

A deep dive on the geosphere, in three layers. The first is deciding what the planet looks like before the clock starts — a held moment, a molding table, a library of starting worlds. The second is the rock physics of a mobile lid: the mantle, plates with a life, faults with traces, volcanoes with landforms, hydrothermal ground, and a record that remembers. The third is the rest of the solar system. `planetKind` now stamps Mars, Venus, Io, the Moon, Mercury and the ice bodies so they no longer share a Voronoi Earth; the remaining work is making each of those maps do the geology that world actually does.

Kind: **321** rock physics, **46** picture, **115** instrument or lever. Effort is S/M/L. Impact is 1–3.

## Where the geology actually is

- **There is no moment before the world exists.** `generate(seed, rule)` runs interior, `generateTectonics`, `initMantle`, `refineEarthHypsometry`, sea level, climate and biosphere in one uninterrupted pass and returns a finished, already-living planet. There is nowhere to stand between “nothing” and “running”, so the landscape can never be chosen — only rerolled. This is the largest gap in the whole document and it is the one everything else in the first six categories hangs off.
- **Every mechanical part of a paint mode is already built.** `localview.js` unwraps cells into a square grid, `cellAtLocalPixel` maps a pixel back to a cell, `unwrapPatch` caches the topology, and `BRUSH` in `god/brush.js` already has a profile, a kilometre radius, hardness, a rate, masks, snap-to-coast, three symmetry modes and an undo stack. The data even lives on six square cube faces, so a flat canvas needs no projection at all. What is missing is a canvas larger than a 57-cell patch and verbs aimed at terrain.
- **Two hundred catalogue worlds used to get the same map.** They no longer do. `planetKind` routes each ruleset into `refinePlanetHypsometry` or a body-specific ice shell: Mars gets a dichotomy, Tharsis, Olympus, Hellas and a Valles-like canyon; Venus gets unimodal plains, tesserae and coronae; Io gets paterae and burial mountains on a stagnant lid; the Moon gets maria and a farside highland; Mercury gets Caloris and scarps; Europa, Enceladus, Titan, Pluto and Triton each get their own ice map. What they still do not get is a measured DEM, and the tick still runs Earth erosion on most of them — so the stamp is a first picture, not yet a planet doing that geology.
- **Nothing hydrothermal exists anywhere in the model.** No vents, no springs, no geysers, no alteration. Hydrothermal circulation carries about a quarter of the planet’s heat loss, sets the ocean’s magnesium and sulphate budget, produces almost every metal deposit ever mined, and is the leading candidate setting for the origin of life — and `redox.js`, 21 kB of careful chemistry, is waiting for exactly this input.
- **The foundation is genuinely good.** `generateTectonics` does real work: Voronoi plates with Euler poles, boundary classification from relative velocity, subduction producing a trench and an arc, orogeny thickening crust, ridges resetting age to zero, hotspots fixed in the mantle frame with an age gradient, Airy isostasy from thickness and density, age–depth ocean subsidence, and ore placed at arcs, rifts and shields. `platesPanel.js` is 444 lines of decent instrumentation on top. Almost nothing in this document is a rewrite; it is all a next layer.
- **Plate motion has an energy source now, and it is four sinusoids.** `mantle.js` has arrived since this document was first written and it is real progress: `initMantle` builds four sinusoidal modes, `sampleMantle` produces `mantleU`, `mantleV` and `dynTopo`, and `drivePlates` now derives `pl.omega` from each plate’s convergent and divergent boundary counts — an actual slab-pull and ridge-push balance, relaxed with `pl.omega * 0.94 + target * 0.06`. What it is not yet is convection: no cells, no slabs, no plume sources, no vertical structure, and a pattern that drifts by `m.phase += m.spin * vigor` rather than being solved.
- **The plate count never changes.** `plates` is built once and `reassignPlatesVoronoi` only moves cell ownership between the existing seeds. No plate is born, split, merged or consumed. The Farallon cannot disappear, an ocean basin cannot close, and therefore the Wilson cycle cannot run — which is why it has been listed as Partial since the first backlog.
- **Every boundary is a Voronoi edge.** Cells are assigned to their nearest plate centre, so every plate is convex and every boundary is a perpendicular bisector. Real plate boundaries are irregular and inherited from older structures. This single assumption is most of why the plate map reads as a diagram rather than as a planet.
- **Crust is created and never destroyed.** At a ridge, `crust[c] = min(crust[c], 0.28); age[c] = 0`. At a collision, `crust[c] = min(1.6, crust[c] + 0.35)`. Mass appears and disappears; nothing balances. `assert.js` already checks water and carbon budgets, and crustal area is the obvious third.
- **A continent is one number plus noise.** `crust[c] = pl.baseThick * (0.85 + 0.3 * fbm(...))` with `age[c] = 200 + rng() * 800`. Real continents are collages of Archean cratons stitched by younger mobile belts, and that internal structure controls where mountains can form, where deformation goes, and what the map looks like.
- **Rock is one byte and it gets overwritten.** `rock` is a `Uint8Array` — 0 igneous, 1 sedimentary, 2 metamorphic, plus 4 for banded iron from `redox.js` and 5 for the K–Pg ejecta from `extinction.js`. Nothing lithifies, nothing is buried, nothing is metamorphosed, nothing is exhumed. The rock cycle has three states and no transitions between them.
- **The core sampler is reading a planet with no layers.** `coreSample` reconstructs a plausible column from present-day field values. It is one of the best instruments in the build and there is no stratigraphy underneath it — no deposition order, no hiatus, no unconformity. The gaps are where most of the information in a real record lives.
- **Isostasy is computed once, and it is local.** `freeboard = thick * (1 - dens / mantle) * (oceanic ? 1.6 : 3.4)`, evaluated in `generateTectonics` and never again except where `resistTick` pushes back on the player. That is Airy compensation — each column floating independently. Flexure is what produces foreland basins, forebulges, and the rebound still lifting Scandinavia.
- **Erosion is one process.** `erosionTick` is a clean stream-power law — `discharge * slope² * 0.15`, capped, deposited into the single steepest downhill neighbour. It is the right first process and the only one. No hillslope diffusion, no landslides, no ice, no wind, no dissolution — so terrain reads as fluvially incised even where no river has ever run.
- **Earthquakes are a counter, not a fault.** `strain[c]` accumulates and releases above 1.1, logging a magnitude of `4 + mag * 3`. There is no fault object, no rupture that propagates, no segment, no aftershock, and no reason for the magnitudes to follow Gutenberg–Richter — which they would for free if ruptures had lengths.
- **A volcano now has a composition and still has no shape.** The eruption code has moved a long way: `silicaForVent` assigns 0.63 at continental arcs and 0.48 at hotspots, `v.vol` and `v.roof` give a chamber and a strength, `overpress` gates the eruption, `visc = exp((silica - 0.5) * 12)` sets how far lava spreads, and caldera collapse, stratospheric injection and explosive-versus-effusive are all in. This is one of the better-modelled things in the build. What every eruption still produces is a height change at one cell — so a 100 km shield and a 3 km cone are the same landform, and no island, chain, arc or caldera field ever appears. Io is the extreme of that gap: it now has dozens of paterae as depressions, and they still erupt as a one-cell height change.
- **Nothing can show you the past.** `platesPanel.js` reports the present state well. The most compelling thing a tectonics model can offer is a reconstruction — this planet, 200 million years ago, from its own recorded motions — and there is no path to one, because nothing records the motions.

## What happens where plates meet — 17

_Boundary type is recomputed per cell from relative velocity, which is the right foundation. What sits on top of it is four `if` branches: subduction thins crust and thickens a neighbour, orogeny adds 0.35, ridges clamp to 0.28, transform sets strain. Every structure a real margin has — the prism, the forearc, the back-arc, the suture, the triple junction — is downstream of a boundary being a *curve* rather than a set of tagged cells._

| 131 | **The forearc–arc–backarc triad** <br>needs `subduct` | Trench, accretionary prism, forearc basin, volcanic arc, back-arc — five distinct environments in a fixed order across a margin. `generateTectonics` produces two: a thinned cell and a thickened neighbour. ## Continental crust and its history — 16

_`crust[c] = pl.baseThick * (0.85 + 0.3 * fbm(...))` and `age[c] = 200 + rng() * 800` for continental cells. ## Deformation, faults and earthquakes — 15

_`strain[c]` accumulates at convergent and transform cells and releases stochastically at `strain > 1.1`, logging a magnitude of `4 + mag * 3`. That is a per-cell counter, not a fault. There is no rupture that propagates, no segment, no aftershock sequence, and no reason for the magnitudes to follow the distribution real ones do._

## Flexure, isostasy and rheology — 14

_Isostasy is one line computed once at generation: `freeboard = thick * (1 - dens / mantle) * (oceanic ? 1.6 : 3.4)`. That is local Airy compensation. Real lithosphere has elastic strength, so loads are supported regionally — which is what produces foreland basins, forebulges, and the delayed rebound that is still lifting Scandinavia._

## Sequencing

1. **First, a moment before the clock starts.** `canvasmode` and `startmap`. Split `generate()` where `refineEarthHypsometry` finishes and hold the world there; make the terrain a saved artifact rather than a consequence of the seed. Between them these two unblock 25 items and they are the precondition for every other idea in the first six categories. Neither one requires a single new piece of physics.
2. **Then the molding table.** `paintmode`. A canvas that is the whole sphere — six cube faces as an unfolded net, which is the layout the data already uses, so there is no projection to correct and `cellAtFace` is the entire mapping. `BRUSH` already supplies the profile, radius, hardness, masks, snap and symmetry. This is the item that makes the feature feel like molding rather than configuring, and it unblocks 22 more.
3. **Then give it somewhere to start from.** `startlib` and `realdem`. Fifteen good starting worlds beats infinite rerolling, and a real Earth elevation grid is a small table that makes every downstream system checkable against the actual planet for the first time. Flat Earth, Pangea and the smiley face are all one line each once the library exists — and the smiley face is not a joke, because watching 200 Myr of plate motion and erosion dismantle it is the clearest lesson in the model.
4. **Then make the drawing legal, and make it argue back.** `implied` and `spinup`. Read the interior the drawn surface requires — plate count from continent geometry, heat flow from ridge length, crustal thickness by inverting the freeboard equation that is already there — and then relax the drawing with silent ticks until the physics agrees with it. This is the half that turns a paint program into a planet, and it is where the feature earns the word geology.
5. **Then the two old roots, still waiting.** `convection` and `column`. `mantle.js` has taken a real first step with slab-pull speeds and dynamic topography; actual convection cells, slabs and plume sources are still ahead. The rock column is untouched and unblocks 18 items, including the whole of stratigraphy and the core sampler’s honesty. They touch different files and can be built by different people.
6. **Then plates as objects, and the cycle they make.** `platelife`, `boundcurve`, then `wilsoncycle`. Plate birth, death and consumption; boundaries as curves with lengths and rates; then assembly and dispersal on a 400–600 Myr beat, with sea level from ridge volume and vicariance arriving on a schedule the phylogeny inherits. This has been listed as Partial since the first backlog and it is still the largest missing rhythm in deep time.
7. **Then the things a player actually points at.** `faulttrace`, `edifice`, `hydrothermal`, `landform`. A fault with a trace gives the San Andreas — a creeping centre, locked ends, a restraining bend that builds mountains. A volcano with an edifice gives islands, hotspot chains, shields and calderas. Hydrothermal circulation gives smokers, hot springs and geysers, and hands `redox.js` the input it has been missing. Landforms with mechanisms give mesas, atolls, badlands and drumlins. Four tokens, 58 items, and almost everything anybody recognises about a planet.
8. **Then make each active world do its own geology.** `planetsig`, then `dichotomy`, `venuslid`, `ioheat`, `iceshell`, `craters`, `exomap`. The stamp is in: Mars, Venus, Io, the Moon, Mercury and the ice bodies no longer share a Voronoi Earth. The remaining work is the process each of those maps is a picture of — Tharsis reorienting Mars, Venus foundering, Io burying itself, Europa cracking on a 3.55-day tide, nitrogen convecting in Sputnik, and a super-Earth that cannot hold a Himalaya. This is the pass that stops the catalogue reading as 200 reskins.
9. **Then make it legible, and keep it honest.** `recon`, `strat`, `geoassert`. Run the plates backwards to the map the player drew — which is the one reconstruction with a true anchor rather than a back-extrapolation — draw the cross-section, and put the budgets, the golden preset maps, the round-trip test and the hypsometric comparison behind all of it. Authoring raises expectations sharply, and the asserts are what let the model keep the promise.

The through-line: the geosphere is the oldest and best-built system in the codebase, and almost every item here is a next layer on something that already works rather than a rewrite. The new thing this pass adds is that the catalogue no longer shares one map. A drawn surface still implies an interior, and a lid mode now implies a geology — Mars is a dichotomy, Io is paterae, Europa is a cracking shell. The molding table is how you teach the first of those; looking at Ares next to Io is how you teach the second.
